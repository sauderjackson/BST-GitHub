Use camera changes to support the conversation and keep all angles aligned with dialogue.

## 1. Choose the shot

### Opening introduction — host close-up → everyone wide → names

After the approved show intro animation, start the spoken introduction on the host’s close-up when the host is introducing themselves and the guests.

1. **Host close-up:** hold on the host for their name and show welcome.
2. **Cut to the room wide:** cut as the host says “I’m joined by…” or the equivalent guest-introduction phrase. Show the host and every guest clearly in the same shot.
3. **Names appear over the wide:** bring up the approved guest/host lower thirds while the host introduces the guests. Keep the wide through the full title window so viewers can connect each name and role with the correct person.
4. **Move into the conversation:** after the introductions and title window, return to the active speaker’s close-up as the conversation begins.

Use the actual recorded introduction and a natural spoken cue for the cut; do not force the wording or a fixed timestamp. Follow [Between Takes Animation Style](https://app.notion.com/p/4166d55232fa808189c5dfdb82f9381f) for the existing left-to-right title reveal, gradient, reading time and shared fade-out.

#### Example edit notes

These are shot cues with placeholder dialogue, not replacement dialogue to record:

- **(HOST CLOSE-UP)** “Hi, I’m \[host name\], and this is Between Takes. I’m joined by…”
- **(WIDE — EVERYONE IN THE ROOM)** “…\[guest one name\] and \[guest two name\].”
- **(LOWER THIRDS OVER WIDE)** Guest names and approved roles/company appear under the correct people; include the host’s approved name/Host label using the established title treatment.
- **(ACTIVE SPEAKER CLOSE-UP)** Begin the first question or answer after the introduction/title window.

### Camera

- Favor the active speaker's close-up and hold through substantive answers.
- Use wides for introductions, shared reactions, product demos, and natural transitions. Don't keep cutting to wide while one person talks.

## 2. Place wider coverage deliberately

1. Use an early wide shot with clearly identifiable guests and host for [Between Takes Animation Style](https://app.notion.com/p/4166d55232fa808189c5dfdb82f9381f). The wide must work for the entire title window.
2. Use wides for introductions, shared reactions, product demonstrations and natural transitions.
3. Hold the active speaker’s close-up through substantive answers; avoid repeatedly returning to wide during the same person’s speech.

## 3. Keep cuts synchronized and natural

1. When removing a long gap, abandoned lead-in or restart, keep every camera angle and dialogue track synchronized.
2. Review each join with picture and sound for clipped syllables, overlaps, picture jumps, clicks and room-tone changes.
3. Removing an off-camera question still needs a coherent lead into the answer.
4. Preserve newer user edits and approved conversation cuts. Do not recover graphics by substituting an older timeline.

Use [Between Takes Editing Rules](https://app.notion.com/p/4166d55232fa808d8183f0507b4a6c88) for pacing and production-chatter rules, and [Between Takes Sound Engineering Rules](https://app.notion.com/p/4166d55232fa806dade1fb1ce41db0f1) for audio joins.

## 4. Verify framing

- [ ] Sequence and actual export are 1920 × 1080, landscape 16:9, square pixels, progressive.
- [ ] Keep the episode’s own frame rate; NeOnc is about 29.97 fps, while Gruns and U.S. Pet were 24 fps.
- [ ] No unintended vertical framing, stretched faces or edge cropping.
- [ ] Check graphics against the actual sequence size, particularly assets inherited from 4096 × 2160.
- [ ] Speaker coverage, reaction shots and dialogue stay in sync in the rendered result.
- [ ] Spoken introduction starts on the host’s close-up, cuts wide on the guest-introduction cue, and holds everyone in view while the names/roles appear and finish their title window.

The original process sets these camera principles without a fixed cut interval or camera-switching formula.

## Highlights before the intro — shared process

Updated from Jackson’s HP review on 2026-11-12. Detailed workflow: [Between Takes Highlights — Before the Intro](https://app.notion.com/p/4166d55232fa81f3a416eedc447d0b6e).

For highlights, add deliberate fixed close/medium/wide changes at emphasis, questions and reactions. Tighter framing is preferred for strong statements; wider framing opens questions. Use synchronized alternate coverage and J/L edits while keeping mouths in sync. Do not add slow scale animation by default. These hook choices complement the longer conversation’s active-speaker coverage; the post-intro host introduction and title-window wide remain in the main content.

**Episode order: HIGHLIGHTS → INTRO → CONTENT → OUTRO.** Place a short, truthful highlights hook before the approved Version 04 intro. Preserve the main conversation; copy excerpts into the hook.

- Choose compelling statements, surprises and authentic questions that make viewers want to learn more. Preserve meaning, context and complete words.
- Use motivated J cuts and L cuts with synchronized reaction coverage. Never overlap competing spoken lines or cut a word to force a picture change.
- Use fixed close, medium and wide crops at meaningful dialogue beats. Go tighter for emphasis or emotion; open wider for questions and shared reactions. Avoid slow animated pushes as the default.
- Use the Epidemic Sound plugin to choose music that fits the specific moment. Keep speech clear and preserve the approved natural dialogue treatment.
- **Hard picture cut from highlights into the intro is the default.** A dissolve was tried and rejected in the HP review. Shape music endings separately; keep the branded intro attack clear and avoid competing beds, clicks or chopped words.
- Keep the established intro-to-content music fade and gentle dialogue entry. Measure the red-dot intro cut from the intro's own start, after the highlights.
- Use teal **00 HIGHLIGHTS**, blue **01 INTRO**, orange **02 CONTENT**, green **03 OUTRO**, and purple graphics. Match clip labels and actual section ranges.
- Save a separate native revision in standard Premiere Pro, verify the opening with picture and sound, and present it for review. Preserve originals and prior revisions. An active export hold stays in force.
